## How to weigh the assessments

- Judge each against the mission, not against the route originally attempted. An
  assessment that reaches the same decision through a different fact — an
  appraisal where the transaction price is sealed, a filed account where the
  press reported nothing — is a success, not a compromise.
- Where analysts agree on the diagnosis, treat it as probably correct.
- Where they disagree, ask which one engaged with what the failed attempts
  actually observed rather than reasoning from how these systems usually work.
- Prefer the assessment that names the specific authority, register or document
  that holds the record over one that proposes searching harder.
- Discount confidence that is not backed by a stated way to confirm the route
  worked.
- **Discount any assessment that supplies the missing fact instead of the route
  to it.** A panel convened because a fact could not be found is the single most
  likely place for a confident fabrication to enter the record, and it arrives
  carrying the authority of an escalation.

## Choosing more than one route

If no single assessment is clearly right, you may run several in parallel. Do
that when the routes are genuinely independent — a different register, a
different language, a different authority — when each is cheap enough in fetches
to be worth the parallel spend, and when a decisive result on either would
settle the question. Do not run parallel routes merely to avoid deciding: say
what evidence will pick the winner and when you expect it.

1. Panel assessment

`Selected paths` must list the assessment numbers you are adopting, one per
line, each with a one-line statement of what it is expected to establish. For
`SYNTHESIZE`, describe the combined route there instead. For `ABANDON`, state
what the overview loses and why it can still answer the mission.

The Decision section must contain exactly one line, and that line must begin
with one of these exact tokens: ADOPT, ADOPT_PARALLEL, SYNTHESIZE, ABANDON.
A short justification may follow the token on the same line.

`ABANDON` should be rare, and when you choose it, the gap goes into the record
as a stated gap. Independent analysts failing to find a route is meaningful, but
it is still your call whether the overview survives without what the panel was
asked about — and the reader must be able to see the hole rather than fall into
it.
